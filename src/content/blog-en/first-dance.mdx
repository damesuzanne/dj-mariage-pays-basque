The first dance is the opening moment of the party: the couple is alone on the dance floor, then the guests join in. Many couples hesitate between a romantic song, a French classic or something more unexpected, and wonder when to place it in the evening.

Here is everything you need to choose calmly, with song ideas and tips for preparing it.

<Guide id="carnet-haut" titre="Choosing your first dance song? Let’s talk about it" />

## Before or after the cake?

In France, the first dance usually comes after the wedding cake: dinner is over, the cake has been cut, and the dance floor opens naturally for the party. The simplest approach is to agree the timing with your caterer and your DJ, and to add the moment to your [wedding planning timeline](/en/tips/wedding-planning-timeline/). Remember to check the venue’s music curfew too, which you can compare in our guide to [wedding venues in the Basque Country](/en/tips/wedding-venues-basque-country/).

## Three criteria for choosing the song

Three questions are enough to decide:

- **you both love it**, and you want to hear it in front of your loved ones;
- **the tempo suits you**: a slow or mid-tempo song is easier to dance to than a fast one, even without lessons;
- **the length is reasonable**, or you have planned to shorten it.

## Ideas to match the mood

**A romantic first dance.** “Perfect” by Ed Sheeran, “All of Me” by John Legend, “At Last” by Etta James or “Can’t Help Falling in Love” by Elvis Presley are wedding classics.

**A touch of France.** For a wedding in the Basque Country, a French song is a lovely nod to the setting. “La Vie en rose” by Édith Piaf, “Je l’aime à mourir” by Francis Cabrel or “Pour que tu m’aimes encore” by Céline Dion appeal to several generations.

**A fun or unexpected first dance.** “(I’ve Had) The Time of My Life” by Bill Medley and Jennifer Warnes, “I Wanna Dance with Somebody” by Whitney Houston or “La Valse d’Amélie” by Yann Tiersen set a lighter tone.

**An easy first dance.** Choose a slow or mid-tempo song whose lyrics you know well, and keep the choreography to a few simple steps you practise at home.

## Do we need dance lessons?

They are not compulsory. Many couples open the dance floor with a few simple steps: a sway, a turn, a hug. If you would like a short choreography, a few sessions are enough. The main thing is to feel comfortable, because emotion shows more than technique.

## Preparing your first dance

- **Decide on the length**: the whole song or a shortened version, with a proper ending.
- **Tell your DJ** the title, the version you have chosen and the moment your loved ones join you.
- **Listen to the song at the venue if you can**, to check the volume and the quality of the file.
- **Rehearse two or three times** in your living room, in your wedding shoes if possible.
- **Set the moment** just after the cake, in agreement with the venue and the caterer, and plan any [wedding games](/en/tips/wedding-games/) for another time in the evening.

So that your DJ has all the information, note down your must-play songs and the ones never to play, and send them over well before the date.
