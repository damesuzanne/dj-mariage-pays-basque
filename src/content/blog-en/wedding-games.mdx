A good wedding game needs very little: a well-chosen moment, rules that fit in three sentences and someone close to you to get it going. The guests do the rest. They laugh, get to know each other, and leave with a story to tell.

Depending on your venue (garden, hall, terrace), some games work better than others: our guide to [wedding venues in the Basque Country](/en/tips/wedding-venues-basque-country/) helps you picture the space. Here are game ideas for every part of the day, all designed to be run by your guests and your wedding party. They work in any language, which helps when guests come from several countries.

## Before you start: three games are enough

The classic trap is to string so many activities together that the dinner loses its rhythm. Pick **three games at most**, ten minutes each, and give each one to a specific person: a witness, a cousin, a friend who is comfortable speaking in front of everyone. And keep one simple rule: nobody has to play.

## At the drinks reception: breaking the ice

Guests arrive without all knowing each other. Two games almost run themselves here:

- **the signature hunt**, where everyone looks for “someone who has known the couple for more than twenty years” or “someone who travelled the furthest”;
- **photo challenges**, a list of small missions to complete with your phone.

## During dinner: table games

Between courses, a table that plays together relaxes. **The shoe game** is a must: a witness reads the question (“Who is most likely to…?”), the guests place their bets, the couple reveals the answer. A **quiz about the couple’s story** and the **forbidden words** game round off the meal nicely.

## With your wedding party: the more theatrical moments

Once the atmosphere has warmed up, your witnesses can launch **the big charades game**, table against table, like in our photo. Think “throwing the bouquet” or “looking for your shoes at the end of the night”. The **three-word speech** and the **“if the couple were…” portrait** are calmer and often very touching.

## For the children: their own mission

Children quickly get bored at the table. A sheet of **little guests’ missions** (count the candles, draw the couple, say hello to five guests…) and a **little guest’s notebook** to fill in keep them busy without an adult having to step in.

## At the end of the night: keeping a memory

The **table vote** picks the highlights of the evening, and the **memory jar** collects messages from the guests, to be read again a year later. Both moments work best after the [first dance](/en/tips/first-dance/), so they do not interrupt the momentum of the party.
