Wedding planning rarely follows a straight line. Some decisions shape ten others, some can wait, and plenty of small things only turn up along the way. A **wedding planning timeline** puts all of this in order: you start from the date and work backwards, so you know what needs to be settled at each stage.

After more than 200 weddings in the French Basque Country and the Landes, I have noticed that the most relaxed couples on the day are the ones who made the right decisions in the right order, whenever they started.

Here are the main stages, month by month. If you are planning from abroad, a few extra points are flagged along the way.

## Where to start

Everything begins with three choices: **the date, your maximum budget and the number of guests**. Together they decide the venue, and the venue decides a good part of the rest.

### Getting married in France: the legal side

In France, only the civil ceremony at the town hall is legally binding, and it requires at least one of you, or one of your parents, to live in the town where you marry. Many couples who live abroad therefore marry legally at home and hold a celebrant-led ceremony in the Basque Country, which can take place in the venue garden, with the same emotion and far more freedom. Check the exact rules with the town hall or your consulate early on.

### 18 months before: the venue comes first

In the Basque Country, the most popular estates and wedding venues for June and September are booked early. If you are aiming for high season, visit and book the venue first, checking its capacity, nearby accommodation, the latest time music can play and the wet-weather plan. To compare options, our guide to [wedding venues in the Basque Country](/en/tips/wedding-venues-basque-country/) gives each venue’s history, contact details and a link to its official website.

## 12 months before: book your suppliers

Caterer, photographer, videographer, DJ: these are the calendars that fill up first for spring and summer weekends. Compare quotes on what they actually include: hours, equipment, travel and cancellation terms. Two quotes at the same price can cover very different services.

This is also the right time to set a first budget, line by line, with a schedule of deposits. For a destination wedding, add travel and accommodation for your suppliers if some of them come from far away.

## 9 months before: sketch out the day

Getting ready, ceremony, drinks reception, dinner, party: draw the broad outline of the day and a first guest list. If you are planning [wedding games](/en/tips/wedding-games/) for your guests, now is also the time to give them a slot in the schedule. Outfits, accommodation for guests travelling from afar and your save-the-dates fit in here too. Guests coming from abroad will appreciate a little extra notice to book flights.

## 6 months before: invitations, menu and music

Invitations go out with a clear reply-by date. The menu takes shape (remember allergies and dietary requirements), and so does the decoration.

On the music side, this is the moment to talk to your [wedding DJ](/en/) about the styles you love, the songs to avoid and the key moments: your entrance, your [first dance](/en/tips/first-dance/), surprises. If your guests come from several countries, mention it: the music can bring everyone together on the dance floor. The earlier these pointers arrive, the more the evening will feel like you.

## What to do 1 month before the wedding

The last four weeks are mostly about **reconfirming**: times, addresses, final numbers, seating plan, running order. And about handing the day over to one point person, so you do not have to answer the phone at your own wedding.

> Leave some slack in the schedule. A drinks reception that runs twenty minutes over has never ruined an evening, whereas a timetable that is too tight puts everyone under pressure.

## The checklist for the big day (and the day after)

The day before, check the rings, the outfits, the chargers and the weather forecast. On the day itself, use the schedule as a guide without following it to the minute. And after the party come the tidying up, the final balances to pay and the thank-you notes.
